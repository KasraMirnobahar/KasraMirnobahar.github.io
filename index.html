Kasra Mirnobahar

I am interested in how digital health, decision-making and behaviour interact to shape mental health outcomes. My work focuses particularly on social media, using experimental tasks, computational and reinforcement learning modelling, real-world data, data donation and neuroimaging to understand how reward systems shape behaviour.

My long-term goal is to better understand digital well-being and to design interventions and applied frameworks that can inform public policy and improve related mental health outcomes.

Jun – Sep 2025Primary researcher, MSc dissertation

Social media and uncertainty

I conceptualised and designed a study examining how the predictability of reward timing shapes social media checking behaviour. I built a multi-component experimental task in Gorilla, including a simulated social media feed where participants could explore the feed, upload their own posts and receive likes.

I led the ethical approval process, recruited and screened 110 participants, coordinated data collection, carried out the full quantitative analysis in R, and wrote the dissertation. The analysis plan, pipeline and task materials are openly available on GitHub.

Read the full project, with analysis and codeAdam Smith Business School, University of Glasgow. Supervised by Federica Farolfi, with Georgia Turner (MRC Cognition and Brain Sciences Unit, University of Cambridge)

Proposed PhD project

Person-specific digital phenotyping of social media use and digital well-being. Research on social media and mental health remains inconsistent, largely because it treats users as a homogeneous group and relies on crude screen-time measures. This project proposes combining data donation, ecological momentary assessment and computational behavioural measures to build person-specific profiles of how social media use and well-being relate. It will be presented as a poster at the Digital Mental Health Conference, University of Cambridge, in December 2026.
