I am interested in how digital environments, decision-making and behaviour come together to affect mental health. Most of my work is about social media: why we keep checking it, and how the way rewards (likes, replies, new posts) arrive shapes what we do. To study this I use experimental tasks, computational and reinforcement learning models, real-world and donated data, and neuroimaging.

At the moment I work with collaborators in Glasgow, Cambridge, Amsterdam and the Max Planck Institute on social media and emotion, and on causal inference with NHS health records in psychosis. Before that I was a research assistant in the Philiastides Lab, running EEG, fMRI and ECG studies on decision-making. I did my MSc in Behavioural Sciences at Glasgow and my BSc in Psychology at the University of Social Welfare and Rehabilitation Sciences in Tehran.

In the longer run I would like to help design interventions and policy that actually improve digital well-being. I am looking for PhD positions and am always happy to talk about possible collaborations.

Proposed PhD project

Person-specific digital phenotyping of social media use and digital well-being. Research on social media and mental health keeps giving mixed answers, partly because it treats users as one group and measures use as plain screen time. I would like to combine data donation, ecological momentary assessment and computational measures of behaviour to build profiles for individual people: how their own use and their own mood move together over time. A poster on this proposal will be at the Digital Mental Health Conference in Cambridge in December 2026.
